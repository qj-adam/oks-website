Support for patients & whānau

You don’t have to figure this out on your own. Here’s how OKS can walk alongside you.

♥

A welcome gift pack

Every newly-diagnosed patient at the Dunedin Dialysis Unit can receive a gift pack from OKS — a little something to say “we’re here, you’re not alone”.

★

A friendly chat

Our volunteers call in at the Unit when they can. We’re happy to come and sit with you before, during or after dialysis — just to talk. Get in touch and we’ll arrange a time.

⛽

Petrol vouchers

Travel for treatment can really add up. We can sometimes help with petrol vouchers when things get tight. Get in touch and we’ll see what’s possible.

🍰

Good Bitches Baking

Thanks to the wonderful Good Bitches Baking, dialysis patients enjoy fresh home-baking every fortnight. A small treat that lifts the day.

📰

Quarterly newsletter

News from the Unit, kidney societies around New Zealand, research updates, kidney-friendly recipes, and member stories. Free to all members.

🤝

Member lunches

Regularly through the year we get together for a relaxed lunch around Otago. Many members tell us it’s the most important thing we do.

Membership is free

OKS doesn’t charge subscriptions — we just love hearing from people who want to be part of our community.

Join us to receive the newsletter, hear about upcoming lunches, and stay in touch with what’s happening in the Otago renal community.

Other helpful resources

OKS is one of many organisations supporting the kidney community in New Zealand. These national organisations have excellent information on diagnosis, treatments, diet and living well:

+

Kidney Health New Zealand

National charity providing kidney disease information, education and advocacy. They join us each year for World Kidney Day.

kidney.health.nz

+

Health New Zealand — Te Whatu Ora Southern

Renal services for Otago and Southland are delivered through the Dunedin Hospital Dialysis Unit and outreach clinics.

tewhatuora.govt.nz

+

Organ Donation New Zealand

Information about organ and tissue donation, transplant waiting lists, and how to register your wishes.

donor.co.nz

This is not medical advice

Information on this site is shared for general support and community connection. Please always talk to your renal team, GP or specialist about decisions that affect your treatment or care.
